# Decisions

Each decision with the options considered and the reason for the choice. When a decision changes, edit its row and
say so in the iteration log.

| # | Question | Options considered | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How does the app connect to the class WhatsApp group? | (a) Read the group automatically with an unofficial WhatsApp Web library (whatsapp-web.js, Baileys). (b) WhatsApp Business Cloud API. (c) The teacher posts from the app through Android's share sheet; the app has its own accounts | **(c)** | (a) breaks WhatsApp's terms and can get the teacher's own number banned; it also needs a phone logged in 24/7. (b) is for business numbers messaging customers, not for reading a personal group. (c) keeps WhatsApp as the place the class already talks, with no risk to the teacher's number |
| 2 | How do we know a student belongs to the class? | (a) Membership of the WhatsApp group. (b) Teacher approves every sign-up. (c) A short **group code** posted in the WhatsApp group, plus teacher approval for anyone without it. (d) **The group code to sign up, and the teacher places every student in a batch** | **(d)**, since v1.0 (was (c)) | Whoever can read the group has the code, so the code stands in for group membership and keeps strangers out; five wrong tries per number in 15 minutes lock it. Since students practise in batches (row 15), someone has to choose each new student's batch, and that is the teacher's call, so the code approves no one. The teacher can change the code at any time |
| 3 | Where do accounts and data live? | (a) Firebase. (b) Our own small server (FastAPI + SQLite) on the laptop. (c) **A Google Sheet, with an Apps Script web app as the backend** | **(c)**, since v0.7 (was (b)) | Free, with nothing to run: no server, domain or card, and no laptop left on. Every table is a tab the owner can open, read and query, and a Dashboard of formulas sums it up. Enough for a class of ten. The costs: an envelope instead of HTTP headers and statuses, one write at a time, and a second or more per call ([v0.7](iterations/v0.7.md)) |
| 4 | Where does Gemma run? | (a) **On the phone** (LiteRT-LM on Android, the same `.litertlm` file). (b) On the laptop server's GPU | **(a)**, Gemma 4 E2B, since v0.6 (was (b)) | The questions and the chat never leave the phone. E2B is a 2.6 GB opt-in download that answered the companion's conversations as well as E4B ([v0.5](iterations/v0.5.md)). A phone with too little memory is told so, with cloud model support promised ([v0.6](iterations/v0.6.md)) |
| 5 | How does Gemma know about yoga and the studio? | (a) Fine-tune. (b) Put the whole notes file in every prompt. (c) **Retrieve** the 1–3 most relevant sections of one notes file and put only those in the prompt | **(c)** | The notes file (`content/knowledge/yoga_health.md`) is something the teacher can read and correct; no training run is needed. Retrieval (BM25 over sections, with Hindi and Marathi keywords) keeps prompts short and lets the app show which notes an answer came from. Since v0.6 the search runs on the phone |
| 6 | How do we stop the assistant giving medical advice? | Prompt rules only, or prompt rules plus checks in code | **Both** | The prompt forbids diagnosis, cures and medicine changes, and sends people to their doctor and the teacher. Before the model runs, a fixed list of emergency signs (chest pain, fainting, self-harm…) in three languages returns a fixed reply with 112 and Tele-MANAS (14416), with no model involved |
| 7 | Which languages? | English only, or English, Hindi and Marathi | **All three** | The students' own languages. The app's text, the class names, the WhatsApp message and the assistant all follow the chosen language. Translations are drafts until a native speaker has read them |
| 8 | How do students say how a class went? | Forms, star ratings, or **five faces before and after class** | **Faces** | One tap, no reading needed. Before-and-after pairs give the teacher (and the iteration log) a signal of whether classes help, without collecting health details |
| 9 | What does the studio keep of the questions people ask? | Everything, or nothing, or only with consent | **Nothing**, since v0.6 (was "only with consent") | Gemma runs on the phone (row 4), so the chat and the health notes it saves stay there. The student can share one note at a time with the teacher. The research consent went with the server that needed it |
| 10 | Which demo data appears in screenshots and videos? | Real students, or **synthetic data** | **Synthetic**, labelled on every image | No real person's data in a public repository. The Insights numbers in the demo are invented and are never results |
| 11 | App technology? | Flutter, React Native, **Kotlin with Jetpack Compose** | **Kotlin + Compose** | Native Android is what LiteRT-LM supports for running Gemma on the phone later, and it needs no extra runtime |
| 12 | How are Marathi answers made? | (a) Gemma answers in Marathi from the English notes. (b) Gemma answers in English, then translates. (c) **A Marathi version of the notes, which Gemma copies** | **(c)** | Measured on 19 questions, read by hand: (a) changed facts in 5 answers, (b) the translation step changed facts too, (c) changed none. The cost: the Marathi notes must be checked by a Marathi speaker and kept in step with the English ([v0.4](iterations/v0.4.md)) |
| 13 | Where does the app run? | (a) **Hosted on the internet**. (b) The laptop on the home Wi-Fi. (c) Demos on the laptop | **(a)**, since v0.7 (was (c)) | The live web app on Google's servers (row 3) answers wherever the students are. The release APK carries its address, fixed at build time. Demos still run on the laptop, on synthetic data (`scripts/demo.sh`) |
| 14 | How does Ask know a student's own practice? | (a) The student's whole history in every prompt. (b) Let Gemma write database queries. (c) **Tools bound to the signed-in student** that return summaries worked out in code; the matching notes and the coming classes handed over up front | **(c)**, with Gemma 4 **E2B** on the phone | (a) grows with every class and leaves the sums to the model: in a first probe it chose the right tool 8 times out of 8 but once added up rows wrongly. (b) could reach other students' rows. With (c) each tool sees one student only, code does the arithmetic, and each reply shows what it looked at. Handing over notes and schedule came from reading answers: E2B often skipped searching the notes, and both models misread days ([v0.5](iterations/v0.5.md)). E2B is 2.6 GB instead of 3.7 GB and passed the same 20 conversations ([E2B](evals/companion_v0.5_e2b.md), [E4B](evals/companion_v0.5_e4b.md)). Since v1.0 a sixth tool gives one asana's history, and the tools give every mood and average reaction in words, never a number on a scale, after the phone evaluation found Gemma misreading them ([`companion_v1_phone`](evals/companion_v1_phone.md)) |
| 15 | How does the studio group its students? | (a) One class list, with classes of several kinds through the week. (b) **Batches** (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Pain management), each with its own days, time, Meet link and curriculum | **(b)**, since v1.0 | It is how the studio teaches. A student sees only their batch. The teacher places each new student, helped by the intake (row 17), and moves students after reviews, each move logged with a note. The batches are a tab the owner edits |
| 16 | How does a student say how an asana went? | (a) A rating from 1 to 10. (b) Free text only. (c) **Four reactions** (not yet, with effort, steady, at ease), "felt strain or pain", and an optional note, per item, after class and after practice on one's own | **(c)**, since v1.0 | One tap per item, in words a student would use. The strain flag is the signal the teacher must not miss, so it comes first in the class review. Everything can be skipped, and answers can be changed for 12 hours after class |
| 17 | What does the studio ask a new student, and who sees it? | (a) Nothing. (b) A full health form. (c) **A short intake**: age, height, weight, food habits, pain areas with a note, and a self-check of six asanas | **(c)**, since v1.0 | Enough for the teacher to place a student in a batch; the batch it points to is a suggestion worked out in code, and Advanced is never suggested. It is health data, so only the student and the teacher read it: its tabs are hidden, and the Dashboard shows pain areas as counts only, with no names |
| 18 | Where does the curriculum live? | (a) In the app. (b) **In the Sheet**, seeded once from `content/curriculum.json` | **(b)**, since v1.0 | The teacher's sequences change; the owner edits the rows in the Sheet and the app follows. The seeded curricula are mocks built from the Common Yoga Protocol and classic texts, for the teacher to confirm |

## Open questions

- **The Marathi notes** (and all Hindi and Marathi text) need reading by native speakers.
- **The teacher confirms** the four mock curricula, the batches' days and times, and the intake's six poses.
- **Phones with less memory** than Gemma needs: the app promises a cloud model, not yet chosen.
- **Consent from the studio** to use its name, schedule and teacher's bio in a public repository.
