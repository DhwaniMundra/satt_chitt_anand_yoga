# Satt Chitt Anand: notes for the practice assistant

These notes are the only facts the assistant may use. Each section starts with `## Title`, then a `Keywords:` line
in English, Hindi and Marathi so that questions in any of the three languages find it. Keep each section short.
Nothing here is medical advice; every health section ends by sending the student to their doctor and their teacher.

## What this assistant is
Keywords: who are you, assistant, help, doctor, medical advice, सहायक, मदद, डॉक्टर, मदत

- The assistant answers general questions about yoga practice, breathing, rest and healthy habits, using the
  studio's own notes.
- It is not a doctor. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or tell anyone to change or stop a medicine.
- For pain, illness, pregnancy, surgery or any medicine, ask your doctor first, and tell Ramit ji before class.
- In an emergency, call 112.

## Getting ready for an online class
Keywords: prepare, before class, mat, camera, space, clothes, what to bring, तैयारी, चटाई, कैमरा, कपड़े, तयारी, सतरंजी, कपडे

- Practise on an empty stomach: about 2 to 3 hours after a full meal, or 1 hour after a light snack.
- Wear loose, comfortable clothes. Practise barefoot on a mat, rug or folded blanket on a non-slip floor.
- Keep water, a cushion or folded blanket, and a belt or dupatta nearby as props.
- Place the phone or laptop 2 to 3 metres away, at floor or knee height, so the teacher can see your whole body.
- Join 5 minutes early. Mute your microphone unless you need to speak.
- Tell the teacher privately about any injury, illness, pregnancy or recent surgery before class.

## Starting as a beginner
Keywords: beginner, start, new, first time, how often, शुरुआत, नया, पहली बार, सुरुवात, नवीन, पहिल्यांदा

- Start in the batch the teacher places you in: usually Beginner, or Pain management for knee, back or neck pain. Two
  or three classes a week is a good start; daily practice is welcome when it feels comfortable.
- Move slowly and breathe through the nose. Never hold the breath while straining.
- A stretch should feel like a gentle pull, never sharp pain. Ease off if anything hurts.
- Use props. Bending the knees in forward bends is fine.
- Progress comes from regular practice, not from pushing. Compare yourself only with yourself.
- Rest in Balasana (child's pose) whenever you need to.

## Flexibility, strength and stamina
Keywords: flexibility, stiff, strength, weak, stamina, tired, लचीलापन, ताकत, थकान, लवचिकता, ताकद, दम

- Flexibility grows slowly with regular practice. Warm up first and hold stretches with easy breathing.
- Strength comes from holding standing poses, plank-like poses and balances for a few breaths.
- Stamina builds through Surya Namaskar rounds and Vinyasa, increased gradually: add one or two rounds a week.
- If you are very flexible (for example after dance or gymnastics), focus on strength and control so you do not
  overstretch the joints.

## Breathing basics
Keywords: breath, breathing, nose, belly breathing, सांस, श्वास, नाक, श्वासोच्छ्वास

- Breathe through the nose unless the teacher says otherwise.
- Belly breathing: one hand on the belly, one on the chest. Let the belly rise as you breathe in and fall as you
  breathe out.
- A longer out-breath than in-breath (for example in for 4, out for 6) helps the body relax.
- Stop any breathing practice that makes you dizzy, anxious or short of breath, and breathe normally.

## Pranayama practices
Keywords: pranayama, anulom vilom, nadi shodhana, bhramari, ujjayi, kapalbhati, bhastrika, प्राणायाम, अनुलोम विलोम, भ्रामरी, कपालभाति

- Anulom Vilom / Nadi Shodhana (alternate-nostril breathing): sit tall; close the right nostril with the thumb,
  breathe in through the left; close the left, breathe out through the right; continue alternating, slow and
  quiet. Safe for most people; keep it gentle and without breath holding unless the teacher guides you.
- Bhramari (humming bee breath): breathe in through the nose, then hum softly like a bee as you breathe out. Many
  people find it calming.
- Ujjayi: a soft sound in the throat while breathing through the nose, used in Vinyasa to steady the pace.
- Kapalabhati (forceful out-breaths) and Bhastrika are strong practices. Avoid them, unless your doctor and
  teacher agree, if you are pregnant or have high blood pressure, heart disease, epilepsy, a hernia, glaucoma,
  recent abdominal surgery, or are menstruating and uncomfortable. Learn them only with the teacher watching.
- Do pranayama on an empty stomach, sitting comfortably with a straight back.

## Meditation (Dhyana)
Keywords: meditation, dhyana, mind, focus, thoughts, ध्यान, मन, एकाग्रता, विचार

- Sit comfortably on a cushion or chair with a straight, relaxed spine. Close the eyes or soften the gaze.
- Rest attention on the breath. When the mind wanders, notice it kindly and come back. That return is the practice.
- Start with 5 minutes a day and slowly lengthen it.
- If meditation brings up distressing feelings, open your eyes, take a few slow breaths, and talk to the teacher or
  a counsellor.

## Yoga Nidra
Keywords: yoga nidra, nidra, relaxation, deep rest, योग निद्रा, विश्राम, योगनिद्रा

- Yoga Nidra is a guided relaxation done lying down in Shavasana, usually 20 to 40 minutes.
- The teacher guides attention through the body, the breath and simple images while you stay still and aware.
- Use a blanket, as the body cools down. A pillow under the knees helps the lower back.
- It is fine to fall asleep; with practice you stay aware for longer.

## Surya Namaskar (sun salutation)
Keywords: surya namaskar, sun salutation, सूर्य नमस्कार, सूर्यनमस्कार

- A sequence of 12 positions linked with the breath, practised as a warm-up or on its own.
- Beginners: 2 to 4 slow rounds, bending the knees as needed, knees down in the plank.
- Avoid or modify, with your doctor's advice, if you have high blood pressure, heart problems, a recent back or
  wrist injury, or are pregnant.

## Common poses (asanas)
Keywords: asana, pose, posture, tadasana, vrikshasana, trikonasana, bhujangasana, balasana, shavasana, आसन, मुद्रा, ताड़ासन, वृक्षासन, भुजंगासन, बालासन, शवासन

- Tadasana (mountain): stand tall, feet together or hip-width, weight even, arms by the sides. Teaches posture.
- Vrikshasana (tree): balance on one leg, the other foot on the inner calf or thigh (never on the knee). Hold a
  wall if needed.
- Trikonasana (triangle): legs wide, reach sideways and rest the hand on the shin or a block. Keep the neck easy.
- Bhujangasana (cobra): lie on the belly, hands under the shoulders, lift the chest gently using the back. Keep the
  elbows bent and the shoulders away from the ears.
- Adho Mukha Svanasana (downward dog): hands and feet on the floor, hips lifted. Bend the knees freely.
- Setu Bandhasana (bridge): lie on the back, knees bent, lift the hips. Do not turn the head while in the pose.
- Marjaryasana-Bitilasana (cat-cow): on hands and knees, round and arch the back with the breath. Good warm-up for
  the spine.
- Balasana (child's pose): kneel, sit back on the heels, rest the forehead down. A resting pose any time.
- Shavasana (corpse pose): lie on the back, still and relaxed, at the end of every practice.
- Sukhasana (easy seated pose): cross-legged, sitting on a cushion so the knees are lower than the hips.

## Back pain
Keywords: back pain, lower back, slip disc, sciatica, कमर दर्द, पीठ दर्द, कंबरदुखी, पाठदुखी

- Gentle movement often helps everyday stiffness: cat-cow, knees-to-chest, gentle supine twists, bridge, and
  child's pose, moving slowly with the breath.
- Avoid deep forward bends with straight legs, deep twists and anything that causes sharp or shooting pain.
- See a doctor before practising if pain spreads down a leg, comes with numbness, tingling or weakness, followed an
  injury or fall, or comes with fever or problems passing urine.
- Tell Ramit ji about back pain before class so he can suggest modifications.

## Knees and joints
Keywords: knee pain, joints, arthritis, घुटने का दर्द, जोड़ों का दर्द, गुडघेदुखी, सांधेदुखी

- Pad the knees with a folded blanket in kneeling poses.
- Sit on a cushion or chair if cross-legged sitting hurts the knees.
- In standing poses keep the knee in line with the second toe, and do not lock the knees straight.
- Avoid deep kneeling poses such as Vajrasana if they hurt. Stop if a joint swells or hurts after practice and
  ask your doctor.

## Neck, shoulders and desk work
Keywords: neck pain, shoulder, desk, laptop, posture, गर्दन दर्द, कंधा, मानदुखी, खांदा

- Every hour at a desk: roll the shoulders, gently tilt the head side to side, and stretch the arms overhead.
- Keep the screen at eye level and the feet flat on the floor.
- Avoid putting weight on the neck (such as headstand or shoulder stand) unless the teacher has taught you in person.
- See a doctor if neck pain comes with arm numbness, tingling or weakness, or follows an injury.

## High blood pressure and the heart
Keywords: blood pressure, BP, hypertension, heart, cardiac, kapalbhati, kapalabhati, bhastrika, रक्तचाप, बीपी, दिल, हृदय, रक्तदाब

- Gentle yoga, slow breathing and relaxation suit many people with controlled blood pressure, as a complement to
  the doctor's treatment, never instead of it.
- Avoid head-down poses and inversions, holding the breath, Kapalabhati and Bhastrika, and straining.
- Keep the head above the heart and come up slowly from lying down.
- Keep taking your medicine as your doctor prescribed. Ask your doctor before starting, and tell the teacher.
- Stop at once and get help if you feel chest pain, pressure, or severe breathlessness.

## Diabetes
Keywords: diabetes, sugar, blood sugar, metformin, insulin, मधुमेह, शुगर, डायबिटीज

- Regular yoga and activity support overall health alongside the treatment your doctor gives. Do not change or
  skip any medicine because of yoga.
- Keep a snack, glucose or juice nearby. Stop if you feel shaky, sweaty, dizzy or confused, and take it.
- Check your feet for cuts if you practise barefoot.
- Ask your doctor before starting, especially if you have eye, heart or nerve problems.

## Pregnancy
Keywords: pregnant, pregnancy, prenatal, गर्भावस्था, गर्भवती, गरोदर, गरोदरपणा

- Practise only with your doctor's approval, and tell the teacher you are pregnant. A dedicated prenatal class is
  best.
- Avoid lying on the belly, deep twists, strong abdominal work, Kapalabhati, Bhastrika, breath holding, jumping,
  and overheating.
- After the first three months, avoid lying flat on the back for long; lie on the left side instead.
- Stop and call your doctor if you feel pain, bleeding, dizziness, fluid leaking, or reduced baby movement.

## Periods
Keywords: periods, menstruation, cramps, मासिक धर्म, पीरियड्स, पाळी, मासिक पाळी

- Whether to practise during a period is a personal choice. Many people prefer gentle practice, rest and
  breathing.
- Gentle poses such as child's pose, supported bound-angle pose and gentle twists may feel comfortable.
- Many traditions suggest avoiding inversions and strong abdominal work during the period.
- See a doctor for very heavy bleeding or severe pain.

## Stress, anxiety and sleep
Keywords: stress, anxiety, worry, sleep, insomnia, tension, तनाव, चिंता, नींद, ताण, काळजी, झोप

- Slow breathing with a longer out-breath, Bhramari, gentle stretching and Yoga Nidra help many people relax.
- For sleep: a short, gentle practice and a few minutes of slow breathing before bed, screens off.
- If stress, low mood or anxiety last for weeks or affect daily life, talk to a doctor or counsellor. Tele-MANAS,
  India's free mental-health helpline, is 14416, open all day.

## Older adults and chair yoga
Keywords: elderly, older, senior, chair yoga, balance, falls, बुजुर्ग, वृद्ध, कुर्सी योग, ज्येष्ठ, वृद्ध, खुर्ची

- Many poses can be done seated on a sturdy chair: seated twists, arm raises, ankle circles, seated cat-cow.
- Practise balance poses next to a wall or chair back.
- Get up slowly from the floor to avoid dizziness.
- Ask your doctor before starting if you have osteoporosis, heart disease, or have had a recent fall or surgery.

## Food and water around practice
Keywords: food, eat, eating, diet, water, drink, breakfast, खाना, खाएँ, खाएं, खाने, खाऊँ, भोजन, पानी, नाश्ता, जेवण, खावे, खावं, खायचे, खाऊ, पाणी

- Practise on an empty stomach (see "Getting ready"). After an 8 AM class, a light breakfast is fine.
- Sip water before and after class; avoid large gulps during it.
- Balanced home-cooked food with vegetables, dal, whole grains and fruit supports regular practice. For special
  diets or medical conditions, ask a doctor or dietitian.

## When to stop and get help
Keywords: stop, dizzy, pain, emergency, chest pain, faint, numb, रुकें, चक्कर, दर्द, आपातकाल, थांबा, चक्कर, वेदना

- Stop right away if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, numbness or tingling, nausea, or unusual breathlessness. Sit
  or lie down and breathe normally.
- Call 112 at once for chest pain or pressure, sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, trouble
  speaking, fainting, or severe breathlessness.
- After an injury, ask your doctor before returning to practice.

## The eight limbs of yoga
Keywords: philosophy, eight limbs, ashtanga, patanjali, yama, niyama, samadhi, दर्शन, अष्टांग, पतंजलि, तत्त्वज्ञान

- Patanjali's Yoga Sutras describe eight limbs: yama (how we treat others), niyama (how we treat ourselves),
  asana (posture), pranayama (breath), pratyahara (turning the senses inward), dharana (concentration), dhyana
  (meditation) and samadhi (absorption).
- Posture is only one part; the classes also train breath, attention and rest.

## Sat Chit Ananda
Keywords: sat chit ananda, satt chitt anand, meaning, name, सत्·चित्·आनन्द, सत् चित् आनंद, सच्चिदानंद, अर्थ

- Sat-Chit-Ananda (सच्चिदानंद) is a Sanskrit description of the true Self: Sat (truth, being), Chit (consciousness,
  awareness) and Ananda (bliss, joy).
- The studio's practice follows this path: steady being in the body, awareness through breath and attention, and
  joy in stillness.
